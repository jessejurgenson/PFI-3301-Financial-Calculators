How to Use This Calculator

1. Enter the Purchase Price or Borrowing Need

For a purchase comparison, enter the negotiated purchase price before any down payment or cash rebate. For a general loan, enter the amount you need before lender fees are added. Use the same starting amount for every option so the comparison is meaningful.

You may also enter a Borrowing Purpose / Problem Title, such as Compare financing options for a vehicle purchase or Mortgage options for a $250,000 home purchase. This optional label will appear as the heading above the comparison results so you can easily identify the scenario being analyzed.

2. Add the Loan Options

Give each option a descriptive name and choose the type of loan. Loan type is used as a label only. The underlying calculation is the same fixed-rate installment-loan calculation.

3. Choose Fixed or Variable Rate

For a Fixed Rate, enter the annual rate that remains constant for the full term. For a Variable Rate, enter the initial annual rate, when you want to assume the rate changes, and the new annual rate you want to model after that change.

The variable-rate option is a simplified classroom model with one assumed rate change. It is useful for illustrating how future rate changes can affect payments and total borrowing cost.

4. Enter the Loan Term

Enter the length of the loan in months or years. The calculator converts years to months and assumes monthly payments.

5. Enter Down Payments and Cash Rebates When Applicable

For loans such as vehicle loans or mortgages, enter any cash down payment made before financing. If an option includes a manufacturer or dealer cash rebate, enter it separately. Both reduce the amount that must be financed.

If a loan does not involve a down payment or rebate, enter 0.

6. Enter Any Fees

Use Upfront Fees for costs paid separately at the beginning of the loan. Use Financed Fees for fees that are added to the loan balance.

7. Compare More Than the Monthly Payment

Review the monthly payment, total interest, total fees, total financing cost, and total purchase/cash cost. The option with the lowest payment may not be the option with the lowest overall cost.

Understanding the Comparison Measures

Down Payment

A down payment is cash paid toward the purchase or borrowing need before the loan is calculated. It reduces the amount that must be financed but increases the amount of cash paid at the beginning.

Amount Financed

Amount Financed = Purchase Price / Borrowing Need − Down Payment − Cash Rebate + Financed Fees

This is the balance used to calculate the scheduled payment in this tool.

Monthly Payment

The required monthly payment for a fully amortizing fixed-rate installment loan. It is based on the amount financed, monthly interest rate, and number of monthly payments.

Total Interest

The total scheduled payments minus the amount financed. This represents the interest paid over the full scheduled term if all payments are made as assumed.

Total Fees

The sum of upfront fees and financed fees entered for that loan option.

Total Financing Cost

Total Financing Cost = Total Interest + Upfront Fees + Financed Fees

This isolates the extra cost of financing the purchase or borrowing need. It does not include repayment of principal or the down payment.

Total Purchase / Cash Cost

Total Purchase / Cash Cost = Down Payment + Scheduled Loan Payments + Upfront Fees

For purchase comparisons, this represents the total cash paid for the purchase over time under the assumptions entered. A cash rebate lowers the amount financed and therefore lowers this total indirectly.

Fixed Rate Versus Variable Rate

A fixed-rate loan uses the same interest rate for the entire modeled term, so the scheduled principal-and-interest payment remains constant. A variable-rate loan can change over time. In this calculator, you specify one assumed future rate change.

When the rate changes, the calculator estimates the remaining loan balance and recalculates the payment needed to amortize that balance over the remaining term. This allows you to compare how a plausible future rate increase or decrease could affect both the payment and the total cost.

Why Term Matters

Extending the term generally lowers the monthly payment because repayment is spread across more months. However, interest is usually paid for a longer period, which can substantially increase total cost.

Calculation Method & Assumptions

Monthly Payment Formula

For a positive monthly interest rate, the calculator uses the standard fully amortizing installment-loan payment relationship:

Payment = P × r ÷ [1 − (1 + r)−n]

  • P = amount financed
  • r = monthly interest rate
  • n = number of monthly payments

When the stated annual interest rate is zero, payment equals amount financed divided by the number of payments.

Interest Convention

The calculator assumes each stated annual interest rate is divided by 12 to determine the monthly rate. For fixed-rate loans, that rate is used for the full term. For variable-rate loans, the calculator uses the initial rate until the selected reset month, calculates the remaining principal balance, then recalculates the payment using the assumed new rate for the remaining months.

The variable-rate feature is deliberately simplified. It models only one rate change and does not model rate indexes, margins, periodic or lifetime caps, floors, teaser periods, multiple resets, daily simple interest, negative amortization, interest-only periods, or graduated payments.

Mortgage Costs

Property taxes, homeowners insurance, mortgage insurance, HOA charges, maintenance, and other housing expenses are not included unless they are entered as loan fees. Those costs are important to affordability but are not part of the installment-loan payment calculation used here.

Student Loans

The calculator assumes repayment begins immediately under a standard fixed-payment schedule. It does not model deferment, grace periods, income-driven repayment, interest subsidies, capitalization events, forgiveness, or changing payment plans.

Learning Support & Methodology

Use these sections to understand the concepts behind the calculation, not just the numerical result.

Understanding the Financial Concepts
Payment is not total cost

Longer terms often reduce the monthly payment while increasing the amount of interest paid over time.

Rebates and financing interact

A cash rebate can reduce the amount financed, but the best offer depends on the financing terms and total cost, not the rebate by itself.

Calculation Method & Assumptions

The tool uses the standard fully amortizing installment-loan relationship. For each option, amount financed is calculated from the purchase price or borrowing need after subtracting the down payment and cash rebate and adding financed fees. Fixed-rate payments are calculated monthly over the selected term.

Amount financed = purchase price/need − down payment − cash rebate + financed fees
Total purchase/cash cost = down payment + scheduled loan payments + upfront fees

Variable-rate mode is a simplified classroom scenario with one assumed future rate change. It is not a forecast or a lender disclosure.

Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Choosing a loan solely because it has the lowest monthly payment.
  • Forgetting that a longer term can raise total interest.
  • Treating a rebate, down payment, and financed fee as if they affect the amount borrowed in the same direction.
  • Comparing loan offers using different purchase prices or assumptions.
