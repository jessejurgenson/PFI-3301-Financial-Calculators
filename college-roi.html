How to Use This Calculator
  1. Enter the education period: Specify the number of years in school, starting annual college cost, and assumed annual increase in that cost.
  2. Estimate forgone earnings: Enter the earnings that could have been received while attending school and the assumed annual growth rate.
  3. Compare post-college earnings: Enter the college and high-school earnings assumptions and the comparison period.
  4. Review the implied return: The tool combines the modeled education cost and forgone earnings, then solves for the annual rate that links that cost to the assumed earnings advantage.
Understanding the Financial Concepts
Opportunity cost

The cost of college is broader than tuition. Earnings not received while enrolled are also part of the economic tradeoff in this classroom model.

ROI is assumption-sensitive

Changing tuition growth, time in school, starting salaries, or the comparison horizon can materially change the implied return.

Calculation Method & Assumptions

The project model treats recurring tuition and forgone annual salary as growing cash flows using future-value relationships. It then adds those amounts to estimate total economic cost. The post-college earnings advantage is the difference between cumulative assumed earnings with and without the degree. The calculator solves for the annual rate that would grow the modeled cost to that earnings advantage over the selected period.

Total modeled cost = future value of education costs + future value of forgone earnings

This is a simplified classroom ROI model. It does not model taxes, financial aid, loan interest, probability of completion, unemployment, occupation choice, graduate school, nonfinancial benefits, or differences in when earnings occur within the comparison period.

Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Treating sticker price as the amount every student actually pays.
  • Ignoring grants, scholarships, or net price when making a real-world college decision.
  • Assuming the earnings figures are guaranteed.
  • Interpreting this classroom rate as a promise that college will produce that return for a particular student.
