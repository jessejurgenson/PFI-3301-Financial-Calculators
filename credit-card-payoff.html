How to Use This Calculator
  1. Choose the question: Select whether you want to solve for payoff time or the monthly payment needed to meet a payoff goal.
  2. Enter the balance and APR: Use the current balance and stated annual percentage rate. The classroom model converts APR to a monthly periodic rate.
  3. Enter the remaining known input: For payoff time, enter the monthly payment. For a target payoff period, enter the number of months.
  4. Interpret the result: Review the estimated payoff time or required payment along with total payments and interest when shown.
Understanding the Financial Concepts
Why APR matters

A higher APR causes more of each payment to go toward interest and generally lengthens payoff time when the payment is unchanged.

Why payment size matters

Paying more than the minimum generally shortens payoff time and reduces total interest, assuming no new charges are added.

Calculation Method & Assumptions

This classroom tool models a single fixed APR, no new purchases, no fees, and one payment per month. For a positive monthly rate r, balance B, and payment P, payoff time is based on the standard amortization relationship. The required-payment mode uses the standard installment-payment formula.

Required payment = B × r ÷ [1 − (1 + r)−n]

Actual credit-card issuers may calculate interest daily using average daily balance methods, may use different APRs for different balance categories, and may assess fees. This calculator intentionally simplifies those details for instructional use.

Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Entering APR as a decimal when the field expects a percentage.
  • Assuming the minimum payment is designed to pay the balance off quickly.
  • Continuing to add purchases while interpreting a payoff estimate that assumes no new charges.
  • Treating the estimate as an exact copy of an issuer’s daily-interest calculation.
