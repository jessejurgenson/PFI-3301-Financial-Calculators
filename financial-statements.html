How to Use This Calculator

1. Enter Cash Flows

Add income and cash-outflow items. Enter each amount as a positive number and choose how often it occurs. The calculator converts each amount to annual and monthly values.

2. Classify Cash Outflows

Choose whether each outflow is a fixed expense, variable expense, or savings/investment contribution. This classification is your responsibility.

If an expense represents a payment on debt, identify whether it is a Short-Term Debt Payment or a Long-Term Debt Payment. The two indicators are mutually exclusive for each row. The long-term debt indicator is used in the long-term debt coverage ratio, while the short-term indicator is retained in the results summary for instructional analysis.

3. Enter Assets and Liabilities

Add the current value of assets and the outstanding balance of liabilities. Classify each entry as:

  • Monetary Asset
  • Investment Asset
  • Retirement Asset
  • Household / Personal Property
  • Short-Term Liability
  • Long-Term Liability

4. Choose the Ratios You Want

Select only the financial ratios relevant to your problem or assignment. You can select all ratios, clear the selections, or choose any combination.

5. Calculate

Select Calculate Statements & Ratios. The calculator will construct a statement of cash flows, a statement of net worth, and only the financial ratios you selected.

6. Interpret the Results

Use the statements and ratios to evaluate the financial situation described in your problem. The tool performs arithmetic and organization, but it does not replace your financial judgment.

7. Start a New Example

Select Clear All to remove all entries and begin a different problem or scenario.

Understanding Personal Financial Statements

Statement of Cash Flows

A personal statement of cash flows summarizes money received and money paid over a period of time. It helps show whether cash inflows are sufficient to support expenses, saving, and other financial priorities.

Net Discretionary Cash Flow = Total Cash Inflows − Total Cash Outflows

Statement of Net Worth

A statement of net worth is a point-in-time summary of what a person or household owns and owes.

Net Worth = Total Assets − Total Liabilities

Stocks and Flows Are Different

An asset or liability is generally measured at a point in time. Income and expenses are generally measured over a period of time. Distinguishing between these two types of information is an important part of constructing personal financial statements.

Classification Still Requires Judgment

The calculator does not know the meaning of an item simply because of its name. Students should use the facts of the problem and course conventions to determine the appropriate classification.

Financial Ratio Formulas & Interpretation

Months of Living Expenses Covered

Monetary Assets ÷ Monthly Living Expenses

This ratio estimates how many months of current living expenses could be covered by monetary assets. In this calculator, monthly living expenses include fixed and variable expenses and exclude savings/investment contributions.

Debt Ratio

Total Liabilities ÷ Total Assets

This ratio shows the proportion of total assets represented by liabilities.

Long-Term Debt Coverage

Total Annual Income ÷ Total Annual Long-Term Debt Payments

This ratio compares annual income with annual payments on long-term debt. Expense rows marked as long-term debt payments are included in the denominator.

Investment Assets to Total Assets

(Investment Assets + Retirement Assets) ÷ Total Assets

This ratio shows the proportion of total assets held in investment and retirement assets.

Debt Service to Income Ratio

Annual Debt Payments ÷ Total Annual Income

This calculator defines annual debt payments as the combined short-term and long-term debt payments identified in the cash-flow entries. The ratio shows the proportion of annual income committed to debt service.

Savings Ratio

Annual Savings and Investment Contributions ÷ Total Annual Income

This ratio shows the proportion of annual income directed to savings and investment contributions. Only cash-flow entries classified as Savings / Investment Contribution are included in the numerator.

Important

Ratio definitions can vary across textbooks, instructors, and professional settings. In this tool, the debt service to income ratio uses total identified short-term and long-term debt payments divided by total income, and the savings ratio uses identified savings and investment contributions divided by total income. When course instructions specify a particular formula or convention, those instructions control.

Calculation Method & Assumptions

Frequency Conversion

Cash-flow entries are converted to annual amounts using the selected frequency:

  • Annual: amount × 1
  • Semiannual: amount × 2
  • Quarterly: amount × 4
  • Monthly: amount × 12
  • Biweekly: amount × 26
  • Weekly: amount × 52

Monthly equivalents are calculated as the annual amount divided by 12.

Living Expenses

For the months-of-living-expenses-covered ratio, this calculator treats fixed and variable expenses as living expenses. Savings and investment contributions are excluded from that denominator.

Rounding

Dollar amounts are displayed to the nearest cent. Ratios are calculated using unrounded values and then formatted for display.
