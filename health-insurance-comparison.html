How to Use This Calculator
  1. Enter each plan: Provide monthly premium, deductible, coinsurance rate, and out-of-pocket maximum.
  2. Enter expected covered medical spending: Use the same medical-cost scenario for each plan so the comparison is meaningful.
  3. Choose the calculation mode: Project-key mode reproduces the simplified class worksheet. Standard insurance mode applies the out-of-pocket maximum to covered cost sharing.
  4. Compare total annual cost: Review annual premiums, estimated medical out-of-pocket spending, and the combined annual cost.
Understanding the Financial Concepts
Premium vs. cost sharing

A lower-premium plan can expose the household to more cost when care is used. A higher-premium plan can sometimes reduce deductible and coinsurance exposure.

Out-of-pocket maximum

For covered in-network benefits, this limits the amount a member pays through deductible, copayments, and coinsurance during the plan year. Premiums are separate.

Calculation Method & Assumptions

In the general model, the student first pays covered spending up to the deductible. Coinsurance is then applied to the remaining covered spending. Covered medical cost sharing is limited by the entered out-of-pocket maximum. Premiums are added separately because premiums generally do not count toward the out-of-pocket maximum.

Estimated total annual cost = annual premiums + estimated covered out-of-pocket medical spending

Project-key mode: The course project uses a simplified calculation sequence. That mode is retained so students can reproduce the assignment. It should not be interpreted as a full insurance-contract simulation.

Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Comparing plans only by premium.
  • Adding coinsurance to the entire medical bill instead of the amount subject to coinsurance.
  • Treating the out-of-pocket maximum as including premiums.
  • Assuming all services are subject to the same deductible, copay, network rules, or coinsurance.
