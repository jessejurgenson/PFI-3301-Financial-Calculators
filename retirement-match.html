How to Use This Calculator
  1. Set the retirement goal: Enter the desired future account balance, current balance, years to invest, and assumed annual return.
  2. Calculate annual saving need: The calculator solves for the level annual contribution required to reach the goal under the stated assumptions.
  3. Enter other saving sources: Add the planned IRA contribution and the employer matching formula.
  4. Find the employee contribution rate: The remaining workplace-plan amount is divided by salary to estimate the required employee contribution percentage.
Understanding the Financial Concepts
Employer match

A match is an employer contribution tied to employee contributions under the plan’s formula. Students should read the actual plan description because formulas differ.

Return is an assumption

The assumed investment return is not guaranteed. A different return or retirement age changes the annual saving requirement.

Calculation Method & Assumptions

The first stage uses the standard future-value-of-an-annuity relationship with end-of-year contributions. The second stage follows the PFI 3301 project sequence by subtracting the planned IRA contribution and the employer match from the total annual saving need.

Employee workplace contribution = annual saving need − IRA contribution − employer match
Employee contribution rate = employee workplace contribution ÷ annual salary

Real retirement plans vary. Employer matching formulas, vesting rules, eligible compensation, plan limits, investment returns, taxes, and contribution timing can differ from this classroom model.

Common Mistakes to Avoid
  • Adding the employer match to the amount the employee must personally contribute instead of subtracting it from the total saving need.
  • Using the matching percentage without applying it to salary.
  • Assuming every employer uses the same matching formula.
  • Ignoring current plan contribution limits in real-world decisions.
